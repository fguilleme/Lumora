# Adaptive Tone Debug Lab — halo and destructiveness diagnosis

24 September 2026. **Diagnostic pass only:** no production Auto code, archived variant constants, image-processing thresholds, or variant ranking changed.

## Scope and measurement domain

All 13 Debug Lab variants, including Off, were rendered on the eight existing photographic images and eight Auto Stress images: **16 images, 208 image/variant pairs**. The full sweep used a maximum side of 512 pixels; six critical images were rendered again at 1024 pixels for the inspection sheets. Both output and Off were decoded through the same premultiplied-sRGB bitmap path, compared in linear sRGB and displayed in 8-bit sRGB. These are **preview-domain** measurements, not source RAW/HDR or full-resolution export measurements. The 100% crops are one rendered diagnostic pixel per display pixel; 200% uses nearest-neighbor enlargement of those pixels.

`|ΔY|` uses linear-sRGB luminance (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B). `|ΔC|` is the absolute change in OKLab chroma. The 2/5/10/20% counts mean absolute linear-Y differences above 0.02/0.05/0.10/0.20, **not** a relative percentage of a dark source pixel. The P95 values are measured with 4096 bins; differences above 1 share the final bin. Shadows, midtones and highlights are classified by *source* Y at 0.12 and 0.70. Person masks, or Vision subject candidates where no person mask exists, separate subject/background when available. Missing subject data is reported as unavailable, never inferred from the background.

Local contrast change is the mean absolute change in the two-pixel luminance gradient. Introduced clipping counts source pixels above 0.003 moved to at most 0.003, and source pixels below 0.985 moved to at least 0.985. These display-domain cutoffs describe loss at the preview extremes; they do not prove sensor clipping or HDR clipping.

The complete 208-row evidence is [metrics.json](HaloCorpus/metrics.json), its [flat per-image CSV](HaloDiagnostics/per_image_metrics.csv), and the [13-row per-variant aggregate](HaloDiagnostics/per_variant_metrics.csv). The per-image rows include all requested tone, chroma, regional, subject/background, local-contrast, clipping, halo, four-border and location fields. The aggregate includes per-variant mean/P95 chroma and worst clipping and border values omitted from the compact table below. The [checked-in diagnostic runner](Tools/run_halo_diagnostics.zsh) reproduces the synthetic and corpus sweeps from current Debug Lab sources. Both CSVs are regenerated by `Validation/Tests/LumoraVisualTestLab/debug_halo_summary.py`. Full per-variant preview PNG sweeps remain local, unfrozen validation output; only the priority visual sheets and overlays are retained with this report.

## Halo detector and limits

On source luminance edges with a two-pixel gradient of at least 0.08, the diagnostic samples the processed-minus-Off residual 3–5 pixels on each side, then subtracts the same-side residual at 9–11 pixels. This suppresses broad legitimate tone shifts and excludes the first two pixels of ordinary edge sharpening. Positive and negative same-side bands are bright and dark candidates; paired opposite-sign bands are counted separately as edge-contrast enhancement. A fixed residual threshold of 0.015 marks candidates. Connected candidate runs of at least eight pixels are counted as coherent bands. These thresholds were fixed for this first pass and were not changed in response to the corpus.

For each border independently, the first `max(4,min(16,min(width,height)/64))` pixels are compared with a stripe two to three band-widths inward. Left/right and top/bottom asymmetries are differences between those *excess* values. Border asymmetry alone cannot separate image content from a boundary-processing fault. The **Halo Map** is a temporary Debug Lab display mode: magenta = bright candidates, cyan = dark candidates, orange = border zones, subdued yellow = strong source edges. Its picker also exposes positive and negative luminance differences, strong edges, each candidate polarity and border zones separately. The map does not change saved edits, Auto, or export.

The detector passes [controlled sanity cases](HaloDiagnostics/synthetic_detector_checks.txt): identity and a smooth global tonal change have zero halo candidates; inserted bright/dark bands around a synthetic edge each produce 208 correctly signed candidates; a paired broad edge-contrast band produces 208 *edge-enhancement* candidates and no bright/dark halo candidates; an inserted left-border band produces a 0.0486 left/right excess difference. A one-pixel edge change is deliberately ignored. **On natural photographs candidate counts have low specificity**: natural texture and large legitimate local corrections can also generate same-side residuals. Even coherent bands are evidence to inspect, not confirmed defects.

## Already-exposed images and intervention

The identity principle is **not satisfied by every variant**. Phase 2 A on `01_portrait_light_skin` changes mean Y by **0.0317**, has P95 |ΔY| **0.1851**, and changes **12.8%** of pixels by more than 0.10 linear Y. Vision-person mean |ΔY| is **0.0183**, versus **0.0417** in the background. The photograph is already reasonably exposed, so this is an actionable **quality WARN** rather than a desirable automatic correction. It also confirms that a single global destructiveness number hides a more aggressive background response. It is not a renderer hard failure.

### Follow-up: Phase 2 C on the light portrait

The device screenshot shows `01_portrait_light_skin` with Off against Phase 2 C. The output does look flatter and more veiled than Off. On the 512-pixel diagnostic preview, Phase 2 C has mean |ΔY| **.0233**, P95 **.0747**, Vision-person signed ΔY **+.0140**, background signed ΔY **−.0081**, and mean signed local-contrast change **−.00539**. The subject is being lifted while background and local edge separation decrease. This is a **quality WARN** even though its >.10 pixel fraction is zero.

The [controlled context probe](HaloDiagnostics/portrait_context_probe.txt) re-renders the *unchanged* Phase 2 C kernel with different temporary `p95Override` inputs. Its normal measured P95 is **.8506**, producing the above +.0140 person lift. With a diagnostic P95 of .48, person signed ΔY becomes **−.0035** and local-contrast change **−.00169**; at .60 they are **−.0005** and **−.00225**. This implicates the existing global highlight-context gate as a major driver of the portrait lift, alongside the fixed local detail/tone response. The override is a causal probe only: **no context threshold, archived variant, or rendering behavior was changed**, and a lower P95 is not proposed as a preset value. The probe is reproducible with `Validation/DebugLab/Tools/run_halo_diagnostics.zsh portrait-context`.

## Edge and border investigation

The strongest single candidate residual for processing variants occurs around the bright window in `01_dark_indoor_portrait` (table above). The conspicuous *left* excess occurs on `07_white_subject`: Phase 2 A left outer-vs-inner excess **+0.0581**, right **−0.0017**, top **+0.0037**, bottom **+0.0005**. Guided is similar (+0.0574 left); Gaussian is much smaller (+0.0085 left). Conversely, `01_dark_indoor_portrait` Phase 2 C has **−0.0093 left / +0.0127 right**, so there is no universal left-edge bias. The 100% and 200% sheets include the white-subject left border, portrait hair/window, backlight hair, landscape sky/rocks and silhouette sky.

Controlled **horizontal-flip equivariance** compares render(input) to flip-back(render(flipped input)): Off, Gaussian, Bilateral, Guided, Phase 2 A/B/C, Phase 4 Spatial and Phase 5 A/B/C have mean absolute differences approximately zero on `07_white_subject` and `01_dark_indoor_portrait`, including left/right strips. Semantic and Combined have only ~0.00003–0.00005 differences, consistent with Vision-map variability. This is evidence *against* an intrinsic one-sided Metal kernel, texture-coordinate or resize direction error at the tested size.

Controlled **mirror-padding** adds 64 pixels at each side and crops the result, keeping the same original global percentile and side length. On `07_white_subject` Phase 2 A, padding changes the left strip by **0.000198**, right strip by **0.000890**, and interior by **0.000010**; the observed left excess is ~0.058. On `01_dark_indoor_portrait` Phase 2 C the strip changes are **0.000008 left / 0.000025 right**. Thus the tested edge padding/normalization effect is too small to explain the observed border-localized tonal differences. These tests do not exclude an untested device-specific Metal boundary bug.

The light and dark portraits are opaque. `07_white_subject` has partial alpha only in its **last rightmost source column** (one of 865 columns, minimum alpha 116/255), while the left edge is opaque. Premultiplied-alpha contamination cannot explain that photograph's left-side band. Both sides pass through the same sRGB encode/decode path; color-space conversion could affect absolute values but is not a convincing one-sided origin in the flip control. The Phase 4 maps are not the only likely cause because Guided and Phase 2 A show closely matched candidates without those maps. The evidence currently points most strongly to **content-dependent local reconstruction / filter response around strong edges**, with the exact source stage of any *visually confirmed* halo still unresolved. Confidence in that broad diagnosis: **medium**; confidence that every marked pixel is a defect: **low**.

No variant introduced more than **0.135%** display-white clipping in any single image (Gaussian); maximum new display-black clipping was **0.001%** among Phase 2/4/5. These are separate from visible local tonal destruction. Chroma changes are small on average but are reported independently; a hue/chroma difference from gamut or rounding is not treated as a halo.

## Validation status and open inspection

- **PASS — technical:** Debug build; Release build; 208/208 corpus renders; new Halo Map UI test on iPhone simulator; Off zero difference; controlled synthetic detector cases; all compared pixels finite in this preview-domain sweep. The debug UI remains transient and no production state changes were introduced.
- **WARN — photographic:** Phase 2 A (and the closely related Guided response) materially changes already reasonable `01_portrait_light_skin`; `07_white_subject` has a large left-localized difference that merits human inspection; several edge candidates near bright windows, hair and sky boundaries need a 1:1 visual judgment. Candidate counts alone are not a confirmed halo rate.
- **FAIL:** None in the tested technical controls. Visual acceptance remains pending; no winning variant has been selected.

The iPhone simulator UI test proves Halo Map selection and a populated metric panel. It does not replace a physical iPhone/iPad inspection of the suspected bands, nor measure GPU timing on device. The Debug Lab operates on its preview and cannot prove full-resolution or HDR behavior. No production correction, threshold tuning, archived constant change, or Golden Master was made.
